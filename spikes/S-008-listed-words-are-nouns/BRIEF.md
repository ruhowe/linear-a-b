# S-008 · Kober's Assumption 3 as a statistic: are listed words nouns?

**Question.** Kober 1946, Assumption 3: words in lists, each followed by an ideogram and
a number, are nouns, probably proper nouns. Barber 1974 endorses it (pp. 165–166). It is
the one of her seven assumptions never encoded or tested here (A-111). On Linear B, where
every word's part of speech is known through the decipherment, is it true, and by how
much?

**Why a spike.** It needs an answer key of parts of speech, which the repo does not hold;
a proxy is available: on Linear B, personal and place names are recognisable by the
Ventris and Chadwick appendices and by DĀMOS's own annotation if it can be reached, and
verbs and adjectives by the reference list's verb, participle and adjective rules. As a
first pass, a cheaper proxy: listed words versus non-listed words differ in ending
inventory, ending entropy and paradigm ratio if the listed set is dominated by names.

**Interesting.** Listed words on Linear B are names or nouns at a rate far above the
non-listed set, and Linear A's listed words show the same statistical signature (low
ending diversity, hapax-heavy) at a matched rate: Assumption 3 transfers. **Nothing.**
The signatures do not separate on Linear B, so the proxy cannot test her assumption.

**Must not be read as.** A classification of any Linear A word.
