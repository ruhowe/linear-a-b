# Findings

Every result and every verdict this project has reached, as dated entries tied to the
protocol version that produced them. This is the only place results and opinions are
stated. The README describes the project; the HENGE files under `docs/ai_context/`
describe protocols and contracts; the `CHANGELOG.md` describes what changed in each
protocol version. None of those three states a result. If a number or a judgement appears
anywhere else in this repository, it should be a quotation of an entry here.

Entries are chronological and never deleted. A superseded entry is marked, with a
pointer to what replaced it and a sentence on what was wrong. The wrong entries are kept
because they are where the method was learned. Git history tracks edits to this file.

Status values: **current**, **superseded by F-nnn**, **withdrawn**, **open**. An entry is
**open** by default and becomes **current** only when it meets every requirement in
`docs/ai_context/result-discipline.md`.

## F-001 · 2026-09-10 · Linear B restoration, Knossos D family

Protocol: restoration 1.0. Status: **current**.

A published neural-network result on restoring damaged Linear B text is matched by
non-neural baselines built from the source database, and the published metric sits close
to a frequency floor.

| ranker | top-1 | top-5 | top-20 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| frequency prior (floor) | 7.7% | 26.5% | 63.2% |
| sign bigram | 41.2% | 64.9% | 85.8% |
| lexicon lookup | 62.6% | 73.4% | 85.5% |
| backoff | 64.3% | 77.5% | 89.8% |
| published BiRNN (JOCCH 2023, partially verified) | ≈48% | — | 78–80% |

2,684 items, 75 candidates, document-grouped five-fold, bootstrap CIs in
`results/linearb_restoration.md`. Seen-versus-unseen word types: lexicon 89.3% → 4.3%,
bigram 55.4% → 10.4%. A single 80/20 split carries ±5 points of top-1 variance.
Comparison caveats in `docs/ai_context/linearb-restoration.md`.

Note added 2026-09-11 (F-024). The JOCCH 2023 paper's own Table 2 reports a bidirectional
3-gram baseline at 44.64% top-1 on series D against its network's 48.34%; F-001 was
written without the full text. So "a non-neural baseline comes close" is the authors'
own observation, and what F-001 adds is the frequency floor, the lexicon lookup, the
document-grouped folds and the seen/unseen split, which the paper does not report.

## F-003 · 2026-09-10 · Semitic reading of Linear A, naive permutation

Protocol: matching 0.1. Status: **superseded by F-004** (artifact).

Linear A words matched Hebrew roots 72.2% of the time against 65.1% under freely
permuted sign values, p = 0.016. Wrong: the real map reached 20.8 candidate roots per
word against the null's 17.4, and breadth correlated with hit rate at +0.65. The p-value
measured combinatorial reach. This is the result the obvious test gives.

## F-004 · 2026-09-10 · Semitic reading of Linear A, breadth-matched null

Protocol: matching 0.2. Status: **superseded by F-008**.

With breadth held fixed, p = 0.153. A positive control of 600 planted Hebrew roots was
recovered at z = +8.4. Base rate: 19.8% of all triliteral skeletons are attested Hebrew
roots. Wrong with it: the planted-root control was too easy, since it encoded roots
perfectly rather than through defective spelling, so passing it did not show the
instrument could detect a real language.

## F-005 · 2026-09-11 · Greek as a known-false control on Linear A

Protocol: matching 0.3. Status: **superseded by F-008**; the refute-not-confirm
conclusion survives.

Under a frequency-matched null Greek scored +5.7% at p = 0.005 on Linear A, where it is
not the language, and Semitic +2.3% at p = 0.054. A band sweep showed the null degenerates
above about eight bands and Greek clears p ≤ 0.005 throughout the valid window. Verdict at
the time: the instrument can refute but cannot confirm. Sound as far as it went; the
set-membership statistic was the larger problem and was not yet suspected.

## F-006 · 2026-09-11 · "Consonantal matching cannot work on Linear B"

Protocol: matching 0.4. Status: **withdrawn**.

On Linear B, where Greek is the answer, the true reading scored +1.9% at p = 0.155 and
Hebrew +2.1% at p = 0.054. Only 3 of 23 undisputed Linear B readings were reachable by
consonant-skeleton matching, because Mycenaean writes no coda consonants. Blind insertion
of up to two arbitrary consonants raised recall to 78% without improving discrimination.
Claimed: the method class is invalid. Withdrawn by F-007: the failure was blind insertion
and set membership, not the method. The diagnosis of *why* the spelling loses information
is correct and stands.

## F-007 · 2026-09-11 · The method recognises Greek in Linear B once built properly

Protocol: matching 0.5. Status: **superseded by F-008** (sampling).

Expansion by the known Mycenaean rules raised true-reading recall from 13% to 90%.
Weighting candidates by lexicon count instead of set membership raised discrimination from
z = −0.16 to +2.23. Six Greek lexicons on Linear B clustered at z = +3.08 to +3.22 with
Hebrew at +1.68. Wrong with it: the word sample was the alphabetically first 600, which
inflated Hebrew; under a random sample (F-008) it is +0.30. The clustering of the Greek
lexicons is confirmed by F-008.

## F-008 · 2026-09-11 · Frozen protocol: recognises a decipherment, cannot choose a language

Protocol: matching 1.0. Status: **current**.

**Linear B, the calibration corpus (Greek is the answer).** Real lexicon against a
shape-matched synthetic twin under the same map; 400 words, seeded random sample; 100
permutations unless stated.

| lexicon | map | z | synthetic twin |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Greek, archaic (53,026 types) | Greek | +2.78 | +0.73 |
| Greek, LSJ headwords (80,435) | Greek | +2.91 | +1.15 |
| Hebrew, Strong's (8,674) | Semitic | +0.30 | −1.01 |
| Finnish (37,305) | Semitic | +2.49 | +0.30 |
| Turkish (27,213) | Semitic | +2.62 | +0.77 |
| Basque (41,512) | Semitic | +2.68 | +0.70 |
| Hungarian (36,707) | Semitic | +2.45 | +1.02 |

Calibration criterion, stated before running, met: Greek beats its synthetic twin;
Hebrew does not beat its own. But four languages nobody proposes for anything Aegean
also beat their twins on Greek text, at the level Greek does. The real-versus-synthetic
comparison detects language-like phonotactics, which every real lexicon shares. It does
not detect which language.

**Linear A, run once under the frozen protocol.**

| lexicon | map | z | synthetic twin |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Greek, archaic | Greek | +3.10 (400 perms) | −0.31 |
| Greek, LSJ | Greek | +2.81 | +0.25 |
| Hebrew, Strong's | Semitic | +2.44 (p = 0.010, 400 perms) | −1.18 |
| Basque | Semitic | +2.14 | −0.31 |
| Turkish | Semitic | +1.71 | −0.55 |
| Hungarian | Semitic | +1.70 | −0.29 |
| Finnish | Semitic | +0.94 | −0.48 |

Verdict. Greek scores as high on Linear A, where it is not the language, as on Linear B,
where it is, because the conventional Linear A transcription uses Linear B values, which
are Greek. Hebrew at +2.44 sits inside the range of the unrelated languages on the same
words. Neither is evidence of affiliation. **Given the sign values, lexicon matching
confirms; without them, it cannot choose.** This is what Shannon's unicity argument
predicts for a corpus of this size with no parallel text, and every consonantal-matching
decipherment of Linear A in the catalogue is a case of the second situation.

Qualification on the positive control: Linear B's values are Ventris's decipherment, so
Linear B shows the instrument recognises the right language given the right values, not
that it could find either.

Note added 2026-09-11 (F-024). Hauer and Kondrak 2016 identify the language of known
texts at 97% over 380 languages from about 1,800 words with character n-gram models.
The verdict here is narrower than "matching cannot choose a language": it is that
dictionary matching under a sign-to-consonant map, on a corpus of this size, cannot,
which is the form every Linear A claim takes. Language identification of text in a known
orthography is a different task and is not bounded by this entry.

## F-009 · 2026-09-11 · Hebrew alone rises from Linear B to Linear A

Protocol: matching 1.0. Status: **closed by F-012**: the rise decomposes into the laryngeal channel, lexicon shape and sampling variance.

Hebrew is the only lexicon whose score rises between corpora, from +0.30 on Linear B to
+2.44 on Linear A. The four unrelated languages all fall: Finnish −1.55, Turkish −0.91,
Basque −0.54, Hungarian −0.75. The shift is well outside its error. Its cause is not
known. Lexicon shape is as plausible as language: Strong's is short triliteral headwords,
Linear A words are shorter than Linear B words (2.92 against 3.22 signs), and the
frequency lists are long inflected forms. The test is Ugaritic and Akkadian, consonantal
Bronze Age Semitic lexicons of different shape, under the same protocol. If they rise
too, the observation is about Semitic; if not, it was about Strong's. Pending.

## F-011 · 2026-09-11 · Ugaritic and Akkadian under the frozen protocol

Protocol: matching 1.0, lexicons added (no instrument change). Status: **closed by F-012**.
The Northwest Semitic pattern is accounted for without invoking language.

Two Bronze Age Semitic lexicons, both openly licensed: Ugaritic headwords from the
Copenhagen Ugaritic Corpus (DULAT-based, 4,599 usable, CC BY-NC) and Akkadian citation
forms from three ORACC projects (saao, rinap, ribo; 7,040 distinct, CC0). Same Semitic map,
same 400-word seeded sample as F-008, real against synthetic twin.

| lexicon | Linear B (false) | synthetic | Linear A | synthetic | shift B→A |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Ugaritic | +1.78 (p 0.050) | −0.58 | **+3.21** (p 0.010) | −1.01 | +1.43 |
| Hebrew (F-008) | +0.30 | −1.01 | +2.44 | −1.18 | +2.14 |
| Akkadian | +2.45 (p 0.010) | +1.55 | +1.26 (p 0.119) | +0.21 | −1.19 |
| unrelated four (F-008) | +2.45 to +2.68 | | +0.94 to +2.14 | | −0.54 to −1.55 |

What the numbers say, and no more. On Linear A the ranking under the Semitic map is
Ugaritic 3.21, Hebrew 2.44, Basque 2.14, Turkish 1.71, Hungarian 1.70, Akkadian 1.26,
Finnish 0.94. The two Northwest Semitic lexicons are first and second; they are also the
two worst fits on Greek text. East Semitic behaves like the unrelated languages on both
corpora. Ugaritic exceeds the best unrelated language by 1.07, against a combined standard
error near 0.3 and a spread among the unrelated languages themselves of 1.2.

Candidate explanations, none excluded:

1. The corpus, as conventionally transliterated, has Northwest Semitic-like word shapes.
2. Lexicon type. Strong's and DULAT are scholarly headword lists of root-based languages;
   the ORACC forms are vocalised citation forms with case endings; the unrelated lists are
   subtitle frequency lists of inflected forms. The pair that scores high is also the pair
   of the same kind.
3. Laryngeals. Only Hebrew and Ugaritic carry the full ʾ / ʿ / h / ḥ inventory that the
   Semitic map lets every Linear A vowel sign expand into. Akkadian has lost ʿ and h; the
   folded unrelated lexicons have only h. Linear A is vowel-heavy (Davis: a 39%, i 26%), so
   its words generate more laryngeal-bearing candidates than Greek words do, and only two
   lexicons can match them.
4. Chance across nine lexicons, one seed, one word sample.

Tests that would decide it, in order of cost:

- Rerun Ugaritic and Hebrew on both corpora at 400 permutations and a second seed.
- Strip laryngeals from every lexicon and from the map's vowel-sign options and rerun. If
  Ugaritic and Hebrew fall to the pack, explanation 3 holds. This changes the map and is
  protocol 1.1.
- A headword dictionary, not a frequency list, for a non-Semitic control language, to test
  explanation 2 under the same lexicon type.
- Aramaic and Arabic, to see whether the effect is Northwest Semitic-wide or Ugaritic and
  Hebrew specifically.
- A different 400-word sample.

Until those run, F-009 and F-011 together say: something separates the Northwest Semitic
lexicons from the others on Linear A and not on Linear B, and it is not yet known whether
that something is the language.

**Second word sample, same day (seed 1, 100 permutations, Linear A).** The ordering held
and the magnitudes did not.

| lexicon | seed 0 | seed 1 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Ugaritic | +3.21 | +2.08 |
| Hebrew, Strong's | +2.44 | +1.67 |
| Basque | +2.14 | +1.66 |
| Hungarian | +1.70 | +1.18 |
| Turkish | +1.71 | +0.70 |
| Finnish | +0.94 | +0.90 |
| Akkadian | +1.26 | −0.06 |

Every score moved by 0.5 to 1.3 between two samples of 400 words. The permutation
standard error quoted above, about 0.2, measures null noise for one fixed sample and says
nothing about this. The between-sample spread is the error bar that matters for comparing
lexicons, and on two samples it is roughly 0.5 to 0.8. Ugaritic's lead over the best
unrelated language is 1.07 on one sample and 0.42 on the other. **The sentence "more than
noise at this permutation count" above was wrong**, because the permutation count was not
the relevant noise. Ugaritic first on both samples remains an observation; it is not a
separation.

## F-012 · 2026-09-11 · The Northwest Semitic pattern is not a language signal

Protocols: matching 1.0 (second sample; Hebrew and Arabic frequency lists) and
1.1-laryngeal-stripped. Status: **current**, as a negative. Closes F-009 and F-011.

Four pre-registered tests were run on the observation that Ugaritic and Hebrew ranked
first and second on Linear A and last on Greek text. Each was written up in
`CHANGELOG.md` with its reading stated before the result existed.

**1. Second word sample (seed 1, protocol 1.0, Linear A).** Ordering held, magnitudes did
not: every lexicon moved by 0.5 to 1.3. Ugaritic's lead over the best wrong language was
1.07 on seed 0 and 0.42 on seed 1. The permutation standard error of 0.2 that F-011 relied
on is null noise for one fixed sample; the sample-to-sample spread is 0.5 to 0.8.

**2. Same language, different lexicon type (protocol 1.0).** A Modern Hebrew frequency
list, built by the same pipeline as the Finnish and Basque controls, scored +0.59 on
Linear A against Strong's +2.44, and −0.03 on Linear B. Its laryngeal content is the same
as Strong's (60% against 63% of skeletons); its skeletons are longer (4.44 against 3.77)
and carry vowel letters (68% with w or y against 49%). Lexicon shape moved the score by
nearly two units with the language held fixed.

**3. Laryngeals removed everywhere (protocol 1.1).** On Linear A: Ugaritic +1.79, Hebrew
+1.26, Turkish +1.26, Basque +0.78, Akkadian −0.07. On Linear B: Hebrew +1.80, Akkadian
+1.43, Basque +1.17, Turkish +1.12, Ugaritic +0.47. Ugaritic's Linear A lead over the best
wrong language fell from 1.07 to 0.53, inside the sampling spread. On Greek text Hebrew
moved from last to first: the "worst on Linear B" half of the original pattern was the
laryngeal channel depressing laryngeal-rich lexicons on a corpus whose words generate few
laryngeal candidates. Hebrew's rise between corpora reverses under stripping. Ugaritic's
persists at +1.32, on one seed, against a cross-corpus spread near 1.

**4. Arabic frequency list (protocols 1.0 and 1.1).** Under 1.0: +2.37 on Linear A, +1.16
on Linear B. 72% of its skeletons carry a laryngeal and 48% carry ʾ, because Arabic writes
long /ā/ with alif and the folding renders alif as ʾ. Pre-registered expectation: it
should fall when laryngeals are stripped. Under 1.1 it fell to +0.90 on Linear A and rose
to +1.87 on Linear B, so it now fits Greek text better than Linear A. Modern Hebrew under
1.1: +0.43 and +0.60, null on both corpora.

Complete Linear A ranking under 1.1, laryngeals removed: Ugaritic +1.79, Strong's Hebrew
+1.26, Turkish +1.26, Arabic +0.90, Basque +0.78, Modern Hebrew +0.43, Akkadian −0.07.
Linear B under 1.1: Arabic +1.87, Strong's Hebrew +1.80, Akkadian +1.43, Basque +1.17,
Turkish +1.12, Modern Hebrew +0.60, Ugaritic +0.47. No Semitic grouping survives in either
list.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| Ugaritic | 3.60 | 51% | 21% | +3.21 |
| Akkadian | 3.73 | 20% | 5% | +1.26 |
| Modern Hebrew list | 4.44 | 60% | 18% | +0.59 |
| Arabic list | 4.40 | 72% | 48% | +2.37 |
| Basque list | 4.35 | 14% | 0% | +2.14 |
| Finnish list | 4.39 | 20% | 0% | +0.94 |

Verdict. What separated the Northwest Semitic lexicons from the others was a combination
of the map's laryngeal channel, lexicon shape, and one favourable sample. No test run
separates Ugaritic from a wrong language by more than the spread between samples, and
Modern Hebrew, the same language as Strong's, scores with the unrelated pack. F-008 stands
and is strengthened: under this instrument, lexicon matching does not choose a language
for Linear A, and a result that looks as though it does can be produced by a dictionary's
shape.

**5. Ugaritic and Hebrew at 400 permutations (protocol 1.0, seed 0).** Linear A: Ugaritic
+2.96, p = 0.002; Hebrew +2.44, p = 0.010. Linear B: Ugaritic +1.89, p = 0.035; Hebrew
+0.43. These confirm the seed-0 sample with a tighter permutation error and change
nothing, which is the point: a p of 0.002 describes one sample of 400 words, and the

Verdict. Neither method names the language of Linear A, and they fail for opposite
reasons. The etymological method is a false-positive instrument: it returns a result
for every language, including the wrong ones, at the same strength as the right one,
and it does so on Linear B where the right one is known. The Kober method is a
floor-limited instrument: it returns one true statement about Linear A, and that
statement survived a positive control with no sound values, a null that preserves
positional sign frequencies, and a size-matched test of the floor; it then runs out of
corpus. The one thing the Kober method found agrees with what the field found by eye,
including the a-/ja- prefix pair at the top of a list that is otherwise at chance.

What follows for the project. Every future claim about Linear A's language made through
dictionary matching on this corpus is untestable by construction, not merely untested,
until a version of the method separates a right real language from a wrong real
language on Linear B by more than the sample spread; none has. The Kober line has one
open question with a measurable answer: the corpus size at which its criterion becomes
readable on Greek text, which a sweep of Linear B subsample sizes between 988 and 3,768
would locate. That number is what the field would need to know before asking the method
for more than the one sentence above.

## F-025 · 2026-09-11 · Packard 1974 read: his alternation test agrees with F-019

Protocol: prior-art reading, Packard 1974 pp. 6, 72–109 and Appendix D, read in
full, with the archive.org text as a check; the two JSTOR reviews,
Taylor 1976 and Wyatt 1976, the only ones there. Status: **current**. Corrects item 4 of
F-024. Appended by the prior-art session.

F-024 recorded, from a search snippet, that Packard found the Linear B values supported by
Linear A's alternations "just over 2:1" against random values, and set that against F-019.
The book says otherwise.

- Packard ran the same test as F-019's grid check: pairs of Linear A words that differ in
  one sign, counted when the two differing signs share a consonant under a set of values.
  His control was nine value sets rotated within frequency bands of ten signs, the design
  protocol 1.0 later reused.
- Result: the Linear B values give 7 such pairs among 34; the random sets average 3.9,
  and seven of the nine reach at least half as many (pp. 76–77). For initial alternations,
  five random sets produce at least as many as the Linear B values (p. 79). The "2:1" is 7
  against 3.9.
- His reading: "we must be willing to accept as fortuitous a significant number of the
  alternations resulting from the Linear B values", and "isolated alternations of this
  kind are of small value as confirmatory evidence" (p. 105).

So Packard's alternation evidence is weak by his own account, which is what F-019 finds at
the same corpus size. C-4d is regraded from Contradicted to Precursor, and nothing in
F-024 now stands against F-019.

What Packard did find conclusive was a different class of evidence. The Linear B values
match personal and place names attested at Knossos more often than any of the nine random
sets, which "demonstrates conclusively that at least some of the Linear B phonetic values
are valid for Linear A" (p. 93); matches with Pylos words he calls "nearly worthless".
Wyatt's 1976 review accepted this ("I find his results convincing"). It bears on F-008's
observation that Greek scores high on Linear A under the Linear B values: correct values
for some signs, together with names shared between Minoan and Mycenaean Crete, would
produce such a score without Greek being the language. F-008 asserted no cause after
F-014, and this is one candidate, recorded here without a test.

Packard also framed his study, on p. 6, as following Kober in "resisting the temptation
to resort too soon to the 'etymological method'", the distinction F-020 drew. The repo
reached that framing without having read him. The ledger now has 46 rows, 10 of them
reached independently.

Why, measured on the full corpus after the fact (read-only diagnostic, calibration
corpus, disclosed). Restricting support to stems whose two forms share a slot keeps 17
of the 57 stems behind -ja/-jo, 7 of 22 behind the -jo genitive, 7 of 29 behind the

the two forms share a slot 21% of the time. Nor do they occupy a consistent *pair* of
different slots: for every top alternation the commonest ordered slot pair occurs about
as often as independent assignment predicts (for -ja/-jo 12% against 6% expected; for
-u/-we 10% against 11%; for -ta/-to 11% against 12%). Under this slot definition,
support type, line-initial, next token kind, the slot a Linear B word occupies carries
almost no information about which other words are its inflected forms. A stricter

slot context, is not supported at this granularity, and the natural follow-up, an
alternation counted only across a consistent slot pair, is ruled out by the same
diagnostic before being built. What Kober used was more likely document parallelism:
the same entry recognised across tablets of one series by its content, sheep counts,
personal names, the place name in the same column, which is an alignment of *entries*
across documents, not a class a word carries. Encoding that would need a
representation of tablets as aligned entries, which this project does not have and
which would be a new instrument rather than a version. Recorded as a design question
in TODO; not queued. 0.4 remains in the code as an opt-in flag; the default path is
unchanged and pinned (`tests/test_kober_regression.py`).

Note added 2026-09-11 (kober1946, read in full). Her context was list-level: nouns in
one list share a case (Assumption 4), an ending recurring across one inscription is that
