# Salgarella, Judson (2024), *Signs of the times?*

*Signs of the times? Testing the chronological significance of Linear A and B palaeography*,
in KO-RO-NO-WE-SA: Proceedings of the 15th International Colloquium on Mycenaean Studies,
Ariadne Supplement Series 5 (Rethymno, 2024), 359–379.

## What it claims

Tests palaeography as a chronological tool on ten signs shared across Linear A and Knossian
and Pylian Linear B (na, di, a, me, qi, ne, ni, mi, ka, ku). Finds no clean diachronic trend:
simplified and elaborated sign-forms coexist throughout the scripts' history, most variants
persist over time, and apparent chronological patterns (such as the simplified forms in the
Knossos West Wing deposit) can instead reflect the habits of individual scribes. Concludes that
palaeography-only chronological analysis is "thoroughly unreliable" until it also accounts for
site- and hand-level variation.

## Our assessment

Undermines the unidirectional-change premise behind cladistic dating (the phylogenetic-systematics
approach of Firth and Skelton, which the paper cites and questions directly).
