# Nepal, Perono Cacciafoco (2024), *Minoan Cryptanalysis: Computational Approaches to Deciphering Linear A and Assessing Its Connections with Language Families from the Mediterranean and the Black Sea Areas*

Information 15(2):73. The title on this page previously omitted the subtitle, restored
here from Crossref/Semantic Scholar.

## What it claims

Visual-similarity feature matching to Carian/Cypriot to derive new values, then consonantal comparison against Egyptian, Luwian, Hittite, Proto-Celtic and Uralic.

## Where to find it

- DOI [10.3390/info15020073](https://doi.org/10.3390/info15020073)
- Access: **open** (MDPI, CC BY, confirmed via Unpaywall; MDPI's own site refused our fetch attempts with a 403)

## Our assessment

Verification **P**. We have only seen this restated elsewhere, in a review, index record or later paper. Re-verify before citing it in print.

MDPI issue edited by Revesz. Attempts 'bias correction' on Linear B values, which the field does not accept. Reports only 'some possible word matches'. The 'bias correction' and 'some possible word matches' wording is not confirmed from the primary, 2026-09-13.

Checked 2026-09-13: confirmed the full title, authors and venue via Semantic Scholar, and
confirmed via Unpaywall that the article is genuine gold open access (CC BY) at MDPI.
Confirmed via a Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University repository record that this is chapter
11 (pp. 215-227) of the MDPI reprint volume *Computational Linguistics and Natural
Language Processing*, edited by Peter Z. Revesz, matching the existing note on this page.
Semantic Scholar's abstract confirms the general method (feature-based similarity to
Carian and Cypriot, then consonantal comparison to the five language families) but not
the specific 'bias correction' or 'some possible word matches' wording. MDPI itself
returned a 403 to both WebFetch and curl, so we could not read the full text.
