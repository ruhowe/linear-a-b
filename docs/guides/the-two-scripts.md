# The two scripts

Linear A and Linear B are writing systems from Bronze Age Crete and mainland Greece. They
look similar because Linear B was adapted from Linear A, probably around 1450 BC. One is
read and the other is not.

## Linear B

Michael Ventris deciphered Linear B in 1952. It turned out to record Greek, several
centuries older than Homer, written by palace administrators at Knossos, Pylos and a few
other sites.

Ventris was an architect. He won by doing years of patient tabulation,
building grids of which signs appeared in which positions, rather than by a flash of
insight. That matters for this project, because the tedious part is what is now cheap.

The tablets are inventories: how many sheep, which shepherd,
which village, how much wool owed. They survived because the palaces burned, baking clay
that was never meant to last beyond the accounting year.

About 5,932 documents survive, roughly 54,000 words. That is small for any computational
method, and it is ten times what Linear A offers.

## Linear A

Linear A is older, used across Crete from about 1800 BC, and has never been read. The
language behind it is usually called Minoan, which is a label rather than an
identification. Nobody knows what family it belongs to.

The corpus is about 1,534 inscriptions and 7,574 signs, which is roughly a long magazine
article in total. Most are small clay tablets from Haghia Triada listing commodities.
A smaller set are stone vessels from mountain sanctuaries carrying what appears to be a
religious formula, repeated with variations at different sites.

## What is actually known about Linear A

Enough to be useful, and less than proponents of any particular reading suggest.

Some things are secure because they do not depend on knowing the language. The word
KU-RO means "total": you can confirm it arithmetically, because the number beside it is
the sum of the numbers above it. KI-RO appears to mean a deficit or what is owed. The
fraction signs form a coherent system whose smallest unit is one sixtieth, worked out in
2021 by comparing tablet totals without assuming any sound values at all.

Some place names carry over into Linear B, which is how we know pa-i-to is Phaistos.

What is not known is the language. The consensus among specialists is that it is probably
not Indo-European and probably not Semitic, possibly unrelated to anything documented.

## Why they share a repository

Linear B was adapted from Linear A, so they share many sign shapes. That shared inventory
is the reason anyone can attempt Linear A at all, and it is also the source of the central
trap, which [why Linear A is hard](why-linear-a-is-hard.md) explains.

The relationship between the two scripts is the research question itself, so
splitting them into separate projects would put the interesting part across a boundary.
