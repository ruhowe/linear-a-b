# A fresh look, with the primaries on the table

Written 2026-09-12 after the five primary sources arrived: Kober 1944, 1945, 1946 and
1948; Packard 1974 in full; Barber 1974; Facchetti 1999; Ventris and Chadwick 1973. This
report does three things the day's work required: re-reads every assumption against
those sources, refreshes the methodology pages, and says what the project missed by
building from precedent as it was retold rather than as it was written. Every number is
quoted from a FINDINGS entry. The experimental half that follows from it lives in
`spikes/` under its own rules.

## 1. Assumptions refreshed

The register runs A-001 to A-132 as of this report, with Kober 0.9 adding from A-140. Three kinds of change.

**Assumptions the primaries turned from ours into theirs.** The value-transfer caution
(A-001) is stated by Ventris and Chadwick on p. 32, by Bennett in 1953 and by Barber on
p. 98. The "match means affiliation" warning (A-030) is Barber p. 209 and Packard p. 90.
Linear B as the size-matched comparison for Linear A (A-032) is Barber p. 212, in prose.
The suffixal presupposition (A-040) is named as a gap by Barber herself: the Semitic
candidates "are also heavily prefixing, a feature which has not been internally
demonstrated for Linear A" (p. 213). None of these is weakened by having been said
before; each is now a stated limit with a citation rather than a house view.

**Assumptions tested since the register was written.** A-002 (dividers) tested by
edition (F-023) and now bounded by the Packard comparison (F-035): a third of a careful
1974 reader's alternation material is not in today's edition at all. A-003 (the corpus
stands for GORILA) now says what is missing: the 2024 supplement and the Anetaki ring.
A-004 (certain means secure) tested (F-015). A-005 (Linear A follows Linear B spelling)
checked against the spelling rules themselves: the coda rule matches, initial s- before
a consonant was never expanded. A-037 (the answer key is complete enough) is the one
that failed twice, at Kober's own example (F-031) and at the whole consonant-stem and
s-stem inventory (F-034). A-112 and A-115, Kober's list condition, measured (F-030).

**Assumptions inherited from precedent and never recorded.** A new section H, A-123
onward: structure at the word's end only, endings of one or two signs, the word type as
the unit, sign identity as the edition's label, alternations as pairs rather than
Kober's triplets, our two nulls rather than Barber's random corpus, the corpus as the
loader gives it, and the divider-bounded word with Linear B spelling as the unit of
dictionary matching. Each names the alternative not tried. Two of them, the medial
class and the homophone identities, become Kober 0.9; one, the prefixing positive
control, becomes spike S-001.

## 2. Methodology refreshed

The harness page now opens with the line's closure and the three primaries that stated
its failure mode before it was measured. The Kober page carries what her papers say she
did, the reading rules found in the runs, and the version line 0.1 to 0.9. The
terminology page gains answer key, floor, edition, exploratory and spike. The discipline
page gains three rules from this week, "read the primary before building", "check the
answer key against the canonical example", "register a method's stated assumptions as
atoms", and two more from today: an answer key is a versioned artefact derived from a
named primary, rescored across every existing result when it changes; and a spike is not
a finding.

## 3. What we missed by following precedent

Precedent, as retold, gave us the triplets and the grid. Precedent, as written, had more.

1. **Kober's list condition.** Her Assumptions 4, 6 and 7 make the tablet the unit in
   which words share a case. We encoded a word slot (0.4) and an entry role (0.5) before
   reading her, then her condition as a tie-break (0.7). It is a fact of Linear B,
   nearly twice chance, and unselective (F-030, F-032).
2. **Kober's Assumption 3**, that listed words are nouns: never encoded or tested (spike
   S-008).
3. **Packard's medial class.** Twenty-six Linear A pairs with first and third signs
   identical; the instrument cannot represent 28 of the 29 transcribed (F-035). Kober
   0.9 adds the channel.
4. **Packard's toponyms.** His real evidence for the borrowed values was 13 place-name
   matches against 3 by chance (Table 14), not the internal alternations everyone
   quotes. Never rerun with a proper null (spike S-003).
5. **Packard's readings.** 67 of his 175 Appendix A sign-groups do not exist under
   today's edition, including three sign values. The edition effect is larger than
   F-023's GORILA-against-SigLA measurement showed.
6. **Barber's prefixing remark and Facchetti's ja-/a-.** The prefix channel has no
   positive control and was judged weak on a corpus that does not prefix. Mirroring
   Linear B supplies the control (spike S-001).
7. **Barber's random-corpus null** (p. 78) and her boundary tests (ch. IX): neither run.
8. **Ventris and Chadwick §9 and §15**: initial s- omission and enclitic joining, two
   expansion gaps in a closed line; §13 homophones, an identity merge not done (0.9).
9. **The answer key**, three revisions from its source (F-031, F-034).
10. **The corpus**, described from the loader rather than from the editions until
    today: 67% of documents wordless (F-035); the "1985 material only" this item first
    said was withdrawn the same night, see the addendum.

What we did not miss: the nulls, the wrong-language controls, the floor, and the
two-edition check are ours and nobody's before; Barber asked for the floor measurement on
p. 237 and Packard built the value-rotation null we reuse.

**The answer key, rebuilt (F-034).** Reference list v3, derived from the morphology
chapter, passed the permissiveness check written before it ran: a shuffled corpus's own
top ten still matches only three rules at the 99th percentile, and chance never reaches
the top-three bar at Linear A's size. On Linear B cut to Linear A's size, "top three all
grammar" moved from six of twenty under the list written from memory to thirteen under
the list read from the book, with no change to the instrument, and to fourteen with the
role tie-break; the bar is sixteen. The largest single lever on the calibration was the
key. The remaining four subsamples in twenty are those where the third-ranked alternation
is a chance pair of frequent endings, which no key can fix and no context encoding tried
has fixed. Linear A was left alone in this phase; its numbers stand.

## Addendum, the night of 2026-09-12: what the items above produced

Written after the runs named under "What changes next" finished. Every number is quoted
from the FINDINGS entry in brackets.

**Kober 0.9 (F-040).** The medial channel sees variation beyond chance on full Linear B,
but the differing signs share a consonant only at the null's edge, and at Linear A's size
it sees nothing on Greek either; Linear A's medial pairs are at chance. The homophone
merge succeeded where every context encoding had failed: Greek at Linear A's size passed
the bar, eighteen of twenty, and the Linear A run under the merged instrument was
unchanged. The instrument has run out of components that could account for the
difference between the corpora.

**Value transfer 1.0 (F-041).** The toponym test, promoted from spike S-003b with two
more wrong controls, met its pre-registered criterion in all eight cells. The register's
first assumption is amended: tested positive for thirteen sign labels, all in the
demonstrably shared set, and a stated limit for the rest. Item 4 above is closed.

**Spikes S-012 to S-014 (F-042, F-043).** Linear A sits with Greek on the Indus-debate
measures that survive the alphabet-size objection, and with the non-linguistic
references only where signary size drives the number; Sproat's reversal, from a real
control. Reversing any support type's words changes nothing on either corpus. Barber's
boundary test, item 7 above, puts Linear A at the bottom of Greek's own range at the same
size: no sign of under-division, and no power to say the dividers are right. S-013 also
found that null draws were not reproducible across processes, fixed as Kober 0.9.1 with
no statistic changed.

**The floor, re-measured (F-046).** Ru's point that the supplement's 427 signs are
everything found since 1985, about eleven signs a year, made the floor question sharp:
if finds cannot meet it, can the instrument? Under the current instrument the sweep's own
criterion gives 1,250 word types, down from 1,875, with Greek at Linear A's size one
subsample short of the bar; the strict top-three bar gives 750. The answer key and the
merge moved it; no text did. The merge is decipherment knowledge, so the lower number
describes Greek with that help. Claim C-5 now names the instrument with the number.

**The editions (F-045).** Most of the disagreement between GORILA and SigLA is the two
loader chains tagging words against logograms differently, and the same sign under two
labels (`*56` and `PA₃`, `*79` and `ZU`); the text differs in about eighty documents.

**The supplement, corrected at 05:20.** Its sign index, transcribed, shows that 79 of the
114 inscriptions it references are already in the loaded corpus and 20 of its 24 complete
word types already in the word-type set: the chain carries Younger's post-1985 readings,
and F-035's "1985 material only" is withdrawn. The paragraph below was written before
that check and its sizing stands as an upper bound.

**The supplement.** Bought and read: the sign plates and the
sign index, from which the word-type list can be rebuilt. Its 427 signs are about five
percent of the corpus, some fifty to a hundred word types against a floor of 1,875; the
reruns it will get are for the toponym test and edition-independence, not for the floor.
