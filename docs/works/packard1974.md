# Packard (1974), *Minoan Linear A*

University of California Press.

## What it claims

Computer positional sign-frequency study, plus NINE 'fictitious decipherments' — random reassignment of Linear B values within frequency bands — as a null, with Ventris values as control.

Added 2026-09-11 (prior-art search): the archive.org copy was reached. Appendix A "collects pairs of Linear A sign-groups exhibiting alternations which could represent inflection... selection follows these criteria mechanically, with no attempt to segregate the plausible from the merely coincidental" (verified Y for this passage). He never scored a wrong real language and ran nothing on Linear B as a control.

Read 2026-09-11: pp. 6, 72, 92–94, 104–109, 124 and 178–179 in full, and the archive.org OCR text (item `bub_gb_vax3kwoscWQC`, poor quality). Page numbers are the book's.

- **Framing, p. 6.** He follows Kober, "resisting the temptation to resort too soon to the 'etymological method'", and addresses "non-etymological evidence for the Linear A phonetic values". This is the same two-method distinction the repo drew in F-020, in 1974.
- **The control, pp. 73–74.** He first estimates chance matches by arithmetic (about five random matches between 200 Linear A and 1,000 Linear B words), then builds nine "random decipherments": signs sorted by frequency into groups of ten, Linear B values rotated within each group, so each value lands on a sign of similar frequency and no sign keeps the same value twice. It is the frequency-banded reassignment protocol 1.0 later reused.
- **Alternations, pp. 75–80.** Of 34 word pairs sharing their first two signs, the Linear B values give 7 whose third signs share a consonant; the nine random sets average 3.9, and seven of the nine reach at least half as many. His reading: "we must be willing to accept as fortuitous a significant number of the alternations resulting from the Linear B values". Short alternations "offer no confirmatory evidence"; for initial alternations, five random sets produced at least as many as the Linear B values. The "2:1" in a search snippet is this 7 against 3.9, stripped of his conclusion. Tables 13 and 14 did not survive the OCR.
- **Names, pp. 91–93.** Matches with Linear B names at Knossos separate the real values from the random ones far more clearly: "The Linear B phonetic values produce substantially more parallels than any of the random decipherments, which demonstrates conclusively that at least some of the Linear B phonetic values are valid for Linear A." Matches with Pylos words are "nearly worthless", since the random sets average almost three.
- **Weights, pp. 93–94.** Each class of evidence is weighted by how well it beats the random sets: "The random decipherments give us a method for judging the background 'noise' against which the alleged confirmatory evidence must assert itself."

## Added 2026-09-12: passages recovered from the archive.org search snippets

The archive.org search-inside results for "Linear A" were saved (362 clean snippets across
201 printed pages). They are cleaner
than the OCR and include sentences the OCR had lost. The ones that bear on this project:

- **His chance model for alternations (p. 73).** About twelve consonants; "one twelfth of
  the alternating pairs listed in Appendix A would share a common consonant by chance
  alone"; predicted "about eight coincidental pseudo-morphological alternations", which
  "agrees well with the results of the random decipherments". Our F-017 chance fraction
  is the same number by a different route.
- **His chance model for name matches (p. 73).** Sixty signs of equal frequency, 200
  three-sign Linear A words against 1,000 Linear B words, a match needing two signs and
  the third's consonant: one pair in 43,200, "about five random matches" expected, "close
  to the actual results of the random decipherments".
- **Vowels ignored (p. 90).** "If vowels are ignored, the random decipherments produce
  hundreds of matches ... The Linear B values produce more but not by an impressive
  margin." The consonant-skeleton channel of F-006 and F-012, stated for name matching.
- **Weighting (pp. 93 to 94).** Tables 13 and 14 give, per class of evidence, the total
  over nine random decipherments, the average, and the ratio of that average to the
  Linear B figure; a weight from 0 to 10 is assigned from the ratio, and per-sign
  "significance" (Tables 15, 16) multiplies evidence by class weights of 10, 5, 4, 10, 0,
  10, 2, 10 and 2. "The random decipherments give us a method for judging the background
  'noise' against which the alleged confirmatory evidence must assert itself."
- **What the appendix holds (p. 178).** Two classes of evidence per decipherment: final,
  initial and medial alternations within Linear A ("most involve a pair of signs which
  have the same consonant but a different vowel"), and parallels with complete Linear B
  sign-groups. The per-decipherment tables (pp. 181 to about 200) appear only as
  fragments in the snippets. Tables 12 to 16 are now read from the full PDF, below.

## Added 2026-09-12: Tables 13 and 14, read from the full PDF (pp. 97 to 98)

Decipherment 1 is the Linear B values; 2 to 10 are the nine random rotations within
frequency bands. "2=" and "3=" are alternations sharing two, or three, signs besides the
alternating one.

**Table 13, "morphological" evidence by decipherment (alternations within Linear A):**

| class | Linear B values | mean of the nine random | ratio | his weight |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| final, 2= | 7 | 3.9 | 1.8 | 2 |
| final, 3= | 3 | 0.33 | 9 | 10 |
| medial, 2= | 2 | 1.0 | 2 | 2 |
| medial, 3= | 1 | 0.11 | 9 | 10 |
| initial, 2= | 2 | 1.8 | 1.1 | 0 |
| initial, 3= | 3 | 0.33 | 9 | 10 |

Per random decipherment the final 2= counts run 2 to 5 (mean 3.9); final 3= counts are
0 except one decipherment at 1 and one at 2.

**Table 14, parallels between Linear A sign-groups and Linear B words:**

| class | Linear B values | mean of the nine random | ratio | his weight |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Knossos name, 4 signs equal | 2 | 0 | — | 10 |
| Knossos name, 3 equal | 13 | 3.0 | 4.3 | 5 |
| Knossos other, 3 equal | 3 | 0.8 | 3.8 | 4 |
| Pylos etc., miscellaneous | 4 | 2.8 | 1.4 | 0 |

His reading (p. 93): the names at Knossos "distinguish much more clearly the random
values from the Linear B values"; the morphological classes with only two signs shared
carry little weight; and the per-sign "significance" (Tables 15, 16) "is not intended
to be understood as a true test of statistical significance."

What the tables say for this project. The 7 against 3.9 that circulated as "2:1" is the
weakest of his three morphological classes; the classes he weighted at 10 are the ones
where the random mean is a third of one alternation. Every class rests on a handful of
hand-collected pairs, 34 at most. His strongest evidence for the transferred values is
not internal to Linear A at all but the Knossos place names, 13 three-sign matches
against 3 by chance, which is the toponym argument and outside this project's Kober
line. The internal-alternation route, which is what F-019's grid asks, was weak in his
hands as in ours.
