# Rao, Yadav, Vahia, et al. (2009), *Entropic evidence for linguistic structure in the Indus script*

Science 324(5931):1165. Published as a one-page Brevia report, with supporting online
material.

## What it claims

Bigram conditional entropy of the Indus script is 'language-like'. ~1,550 lines / ~7,000 sign tokens, a corpus scale close to Linear A's.

## Where to find it

- DOI [10.1126/science.1170391](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1170391)

## Our assessment

Verification **Y**. We fetched and read the primary source.

Checked 2026-09-13: read the full text and supporting online material from Rajesh Rao's
own page at the University of Washington. Corrected the venue from "Science 324" to
"Science 324(5931):1165" (a one-page Brevia report), confirmed against Crossref. The
corpus-scale claim of "~1,550 lines / ~7,000 sign tokens" is confirmed: the supporting
material states the Indus dataset used is "1548 lines of text, with 7000 sign occurrences
in total." The measure is confirmed to be pairwise (bigram) conditional entropy, P(j|i)
for immediately adjacent tokens, computed with modified Kneser-Ney smoothing. The paper's
conclusion is that Indus conditional entropy "closely matches those of linguistic systems
and remains far from nonlinguistic systems," which supports this page's paraphrase that
the result reads as language-like, though that exact phrase is not a quotation from the
paper and should not be presented as one. Access corrected from paywalled to open, and the
standing of "contested" is left as is, since the paper's own conclusion is that the result
raises the probability of the linguistic hypothesis rather than settling it, and the
1548-line, 7000-token figure is a filtered subset of Mahadevan's concordance, not the full
published Indus corpus.
