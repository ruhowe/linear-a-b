# S-009 · Edition concordance: where GORILA and SigLA disagree, as a resource

**Question.** F-023 found 508 of 697 aligned documents differ between the two editions:
13 in divider placement alone, 137 in sign readings, 365 in line coverage. F-035 found 67
of Packard's 175 sign-groups absent under GORILA. Nobody publishes a document-by-document
concordance of where the editions disagree and how. Can one be produced as an aggregate
resource without redistributing either edition?

**Why a spike.** It is a data product, not a test. Licences (both CC BY-NC-SA) permit
derived aggregates; a concordance that lists, per document, the *kind* and *count* of
disagreement (not the text) is aggregate. A finer product listing the differing readings
would be a derivative of both editions and would need their terms checked first.

**Interesting.** The concordance shows disagreement concentrated in identifiable sites,
supports or sign classes, which tells any structural test where its edition sensitivity
lives. **Nothing.** Disagreement is diffuse.

**Must not be read as.** A judgement on which edition is right anywhere.
