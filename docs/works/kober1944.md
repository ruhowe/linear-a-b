# Kober (1944), *The Scripts of Pre-Hellenic Greece*

A survey for classics teachers, written two years before the triplets paper. Read from
the three printed pages; not a summary from a secondary source.

## What it says

- **The material.** Six groups of Aegean inscriptions are listed with counts. Linear
  Class A: "several hundred" inscriptions, "over 100" signs, clay and stone, "inventories,
  dedications". Linear Class B: about 2,500 inscriptions, about 60 signs, Knossos only,
  inventories. Pylos: about 600, few yet published. Then the sentence that fixes her
  corpus: **"About 200 inscriptions of Linear Class B have been published. It is with
  these, therefore, that anyone who attempts to decipher the inscriptions must begin."**
- **Why Linear B first.** Only Linear B and Pylos are "homogeneous geographically and
  chronologically"; the other groups mix places and periods and "cannot be handled in the
  present state of our knowledge". Pylos is set aside as unpublished.
- **What can and cannot help.** No bilingual exists. Pre-Hellenic place names and
  suffixes in Greek (-inthos, -ssos, -eus), Hesychius glosses, and the Egyptian Kefti
  papyri are noted as clues "of no use as yet". The Cypriote syllabary is the structural
  analogue: about 55 signs, five vowel signs, the rest consonant plus vowel; no
  distinction of voiced, voiceless and aspirated; final consonants written with a dead
  vowel (*se* for final *s*); "elaborate rules" for clusters. She adds that this helps
  only "provided it can be shown that the language of pre-Hellenic Cyprus was also the
  language of pre-Hellenic Crete".
- **The verdict of 1944.** "At this time, therefore, we can say only one thing with
  certainty: the ancient Greeks did the Cretans an injustice."

## What it verifies for this project

- The figure the prior-art search recorded from a secondary account (F-024, C-5b), that
  Kober worked from about 200 tablets, is **verified from her own words as about 200
  published Linear B inscriptions**, the corpus she says the work must begin from. That
  is the material behind the 1946 triplets, two years later.
- The Cypriote analogy is the assumption behind her grid inference and behind our
  reference to it (A-040 and the stage 2 design): a syllabary of consonant plus vowel
  signs, in which two signs can share a consonant. She states it as an assumption to be
  shown, not as a fact.
- The dead-vowel and cluster rules she takes from Cypriote are the same orthographic
  rules whose encoding rescued the etymological method on Linear B (F-007), which she
  had in view before any value was known.

## Our assessment

A primary statement of scope and method from the person the method is named after.
Nothing in it is a result. Its value here is the corpus figure and the explicit list of
what she considered admissible evidence in 1944, which is narrower than what most later
decipherment attempts admit.
