# Kessler (2001), *The Significance of Word Lists*

CSLI Publications, Stanford.

## What it claims

Permutation-based significance tests for word-list comparison between languages. Argues that
common rules of thumb, such as multilateral comparison, lower the power of quantitative tests.

## Where to find it

- ISBN 978-1-57586-299-6
- Access: **paywalled**

## Our assessment

Verification **P**. We have only seen this restated elsewhere, in a review, index record or
later paper. Re-verify before citing it in print.

The general-case precedent that procedure and list choices move significance, which is what
C-2 measured for dictionary format on Linear A.

Graded in the prior-art ledger of [claims.md](../ai_context/claims.md).

Checked 2026-09-13: no local or open copy of the book itself was found. The OpenLibrary
ISBN record (https://openlibrary.org/isbn/9781575862996) confirms the title, sole author
Brett Kessler, publisher CSLI Publications, Stanford, 2001, x + 277 pages, based on the
author's Stanford PhD thesis, and confirms ISBN 978-1-57586-299-6 resolves to this exact
work. The full title carries a subtitle not on this page, "Statistical Tests for
Investigating Historical Connections Between Languages," and the CSLI series is
"Dissertations in Linguistics." The claim that the book argues common rules of thumb such
as multilateral comparison lower the power of quantitative tests is confirmed by the
publisher's own description, repeated on Amazon and Google Books: "Many rules of thumb
invoked by linguists in order to obviate chance resemblance, such as multilateral
comparison and emphasizing grammar over vocabulary, are shown to actually decrease the
power of quantitative tests." This is a publisher description, not the book's own text, so
the flag moves from S to P rather than Y.
