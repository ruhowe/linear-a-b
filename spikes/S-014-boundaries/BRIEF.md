# S-014 · Are the dividers where the structure says boundaries are?

**Question.** Barber 1974, chapter IX, gives boundary statistics: a sign pair whose
observed frequency is far below the product of its marginals (a factor of six or more,
calibrated on her Linear B corpus) is a likely word boundary. Applied inside Linear A's
divider-bounded words, do predicted boundaries fall where the dividers are, or inside
words, at a rate different from Linear B's?

**Why a spike.** A-002 has been tested by edition (F-023) and by a 1974 reader's
readings (F-035), never by a within-corpus statistic. Barber's is the standard one.

**Interesting.** Linear A shows more within-word predicted boundaries than Linear B at
matched size, beyond the subsample spread: the dividers may under-segment, and the
stem-and-ending model's word unit is in question. **Nothing.** The rates match.

**Must not be read as.** A re-segmentation of any text.
