# Facchetti (1999), *Non-onomastic elements in Linear A*

Kadmos 38:121–136. Read in full. A philological catalogue, not a statistical study: the
companion to his "Statistical data and morphematic elements in Linear A" in the same
volume (pp. 1–11), which remains unread here and is the paper that decides claims atom
C-4a. This one's appendix carries corrigenda to that paper (p. 3 line 26: "and 25%" to
read "and 73%"), which is the only trace of its numbers available to us.

## What it does

Collects the Linear A words that are not toponyms or anthroponyms, by their positions in
the archive documents (words before commodity logograms, headings, final sections with
totals), on other supports, and in the libation formulae. Sections: isolated words in the
Hagia Triada tablets with a suggested function for each (kind of food, concerning wheat,
qualification of some people); headings and short sentences; inscriptions on vessels and
other supports; the libation formulae, with a full synoptic table of the formula texts
(pp. 133–135) aligned by slot.

Method throughout is by eye and by context: a word is non-onomastic because of where it
sits relative to logograms and numerals, and a variant is recognised when the same word
appears with a different ending in the same slot of a parallel text. On the libation
formula he sets out a basic usual formula with variants in each slot (§ 3.2.1): first
section a-ta-i-jo-wa-ja / a-ta-i-jo-wa-e / ja-ta-i-jo-u-ja; second section
u-na-ka-na-si / u-na-ru-ka-na-ti / u-na-ka-na-ja-si; the name ja-sa-sa-ra-me / a-sa-sa-ra-me
(footnote 73 calls the alternation ja-/a- "attested in securely connected context").
He proposes -si / -ti as a suffix alternation and -ja / -e likewise, and reads i-pi-na-ma
/ si-ru-te as a verb-like pair, all tentatively.

## What it verifies for this project

- **The a-/ja- prefix pair** the instrument put at the top of Linear A's prefix list at
  support 3, at the chance level (F-019), is Facchetti's alternation ja-sa-sa-ra-me /
  a-sa-sa-ra-me and ja-ta-i-jo-u-ja / a-ta-i-jo-wa-ja, found by eye in the formula and
  stated as secure. The instrument sees what he saw and cannot give it a level above
  chance; he does not attempt one.
- **His slot-alignment of the formula texts** (pp. 133–135) is the hand-built form of the
  entry alignment the context line tried to encode (F-028, F-032): parallel texts laid
  out by slot, variants read across the column. It is done for one formula on about
  thirty inscriptions.
- **His conclusion that few vocabulary words are recoverable** because the tablets are
  mostly names and logograms is the limit attached to F-019 (A-030): the stem-sharing
  result cannot separate inflection from names and formula words.

## Our assessment

The by-eye, context-based reading of Linear A morphology at its most careful, and the
right precursor for C-4a's "Stated" grade: it identifies the affixes, does not count them
against chance, and says so. It does not settle whether anyone tested Linear A morphology
statistically before this project; that is the other 1999 paper, and its corrigendum here
shows it did carry percentages.
