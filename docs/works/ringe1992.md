# Ringe (1992), *On Calculating the Factor of Chance in Language Comparison*

Transactions of the American Philosophical Society 82(1):1-110. A book-length monograph
occupying the whole part, not a short article.

## What it claims

A probabilistic method for how many lexical matches between two word lists chance alone
produces, so that resemblance between unrelated languages can be priced.

## Where to find it

- DOI [10.2307/1006563](https://doi.org/10.2307/1006563)
- Access: **paywalled**

## Our assessment

Verification **P**. We have only seen this restated elsewhere, in a review, index record or
later paper. Re-verify before citing it in print.

The historical-linguistics precedent for pricing chance matches. It has never been applied to
an Aegean script or to sign-to-sound dictionary matching.

Graded in the prior-art ledger of [claims.md](../ai_context/claims.md).

Checked 2026-09-13: JSTOR's own item record (stable URL 1006563) confirms the title, sole
author Donald A. Ringe Jr., and that this is Volume 82, Part 1 (1992) of the Transactions,
pp. 1-110, corrected here from a venue that gave no page range. We did not obtain the
substantive text of the monograph through any of the routes open to us: JSTOR itself sits
behind the paywall past the citation page, and we did not use the paywall-circumvention

constraints. The "What it claims" description of a probabilistic method for pricing chance
