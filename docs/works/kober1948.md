# Kober (1948), *The Minoan Scripts: Fact and Theory*

Read in full from the JSTOR PDF. Twenty-two pages: a survey of the six "Minoan"
scripts, what is published and how reliably, the Myres comparative sign lists for A and
B reproduced as figs. 6 and 7, six sample paradigms (fig. 8), possible verb forms
(fig. 9), a tentative phonetic pattern (fig. 10), and a closing argument on method. See
also [kober1946](kober1946.md), [kober1944](kober1944.md), [kober1945](kober1945.md).

## What it says that bears on this project

**Corpus sizes, her count.** About 1,800 Linear B inscriptions extant, "over 200"
published, "largely in the form of transcriptions"; "about 700 different sign groups in
B, and about 400 in A, if we consider only published material." Average B word three
or four signs; only 24 words of six signs and 6 of seven.

**Method.** "The problem is to decide first, whether a variation in the spelling of two
similar words is due to inflection or is merely coincidental, and second, if it is due
to inflection, what kind of inflection. Great care must be exercised ... to make sure
that the contexts of the words concerned justify the assumption that we are dealing
with inflection." The pattern she describes: a word of form ABCD in one kind of list,
ABCE where other terminations predominate, and ABX "in specific positions on certain
palm-leaf tablets." Six paradigms in fig. 8, types A to F; "everything that has been
said above about inflection is extremely tentative."

**The grid, with its assumptions numbered.** Fig. 10 rests on three: a simple
consonant-vowel syllabary "not proven, nor provable at present"; the stem includes the
consonant of the penultimate sign; the same vowel across the first five columns in
cases I and II, another in case III. "Since all three assumptions are unprovable, fig.
10 has no validity. If, however, ... the phonetic relationships are mutually
confirmatory, the validity of the original assumptions might be proven by this very
fact."

**Linear A, in her words.** "Inflection of the type so noticeable in B does not seem to
exist in A. It is seldom possible to find words in A which may have the same stem, and
when one does (cf. fig. 3) the variations are of an entirely different type." Fig. 3 is
the five related words of religious connotation (the libation-formula word). Her
conclusion: "The only safe course at present is to proceed on the assumption that each
of the scripts records a different language." The word for "sum" differs between A and
B; at most 15 identical words out of about 700 and 400; the 50 pairs sharing two or
three signs are dismissed with "gentian and gentile; convection and convention."

**Against value-guessing.** Attempts to read Minoan by assigning Cypriote values on
sign shape: "when we say 30/120 of Cypriote is equal to, or similar to, 30/300 of
Minoan, we are saying very little." And: "Forty years of attempts to decipher Minoan by
guessing at one or the other, or both, have proved that such a procedure is useless."

**The programme.** Decipher one script, Linear B, by working out its inflection pattern;
that pattern either points to a language group or fixes the phonetic relations of the
signs; then use it on the others. "When we have the facts, certain conclusions will be
almost inevitable. Until we have them, no conclusions are possible."

## What it verifies for this project

- The 1944 corpus figure (over 200 published inscriptions) is repeated, and the word-type
  count is given: about 700 in B. See [kober1946](kober1946.md) for the consequence.
- Her 1948 view that Linear A shows less inflection than Linear B, and of a different
  type, is what F-019 and F-022 measured in 2026: Linear A's paradigm structure at or
  below the low end of Greek's at matched size, with alternation support at chance. A
  dated note is on F-019.
- Her arithmetic against chance sign resemblance is the argument the project made
  against dictionary matching (F-008, F-012), seventy-eight years earlier and in one
  sentence.
- Her three grid assumptions are A-040 and the stage 2 design; her caveat is the
  reason stage 2 is checked against the known values rather than asserted.

## Our assessment

A primary statement of the method's assumptions, scope, and limits by its author, more
cautious than most later summaries of her. The project's instrument stands in the
relation to it that she asked for: the inflection pattern first, the phonetic relations
checked against it, and no value guessed.
