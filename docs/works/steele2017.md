# Steele, Meißner (2017), *From Linear B to Linear A: the problem of the backward projection of sound values*

Steele (ed.), *Understanding Relations Between Scripts: The Aegean Writing Systems*, Oxbow, 93–110 (chapter 6).

## What it claims

That the backward projection of Linear B sound values onto Linear A is legitimate in principle
and as an approximation, on the strength of several independent lines of evidence: the script
correspondence itself (Linear B descends directly from Linear A, with sign identity risen from
60% to about 72% of 89 signs as new finds accumulate), a chain through Cypro-Minoan and the
Cypriot Syllabary (eleven signs sharing shape and phonetic value with a high degree of
certainty), Linear A and Linear B place-name equations (15 to 20 shared signs, including a
whole consonantal series), morphological alternations within Linear A sign sequences,
Packard's statistical tests of the fit between Ventris's values and Linear A word frequencies,
and the acrophonic use of syllabograms as ideograms in both scripts. The paper is explicit that
this legitimises reading the sounds, not the language: applying Linear B values to Linear A
yields a language whose grammar and content remain largely opaque, and the chapter closes with
a caution from Carian, where shared sign shapes did not guarantee shared sound values for every
sign.

## Our assessment

The paper's own conclusion, stated in its final section, is that "in principle the backward
projection of sound values from Linear B to Linear A must be regarded as legitimate." This
repo's separate stance, that a language claim built on those projected values is circular, is
our position (CLAUDE.md, `docs/ai_context/corpus-sources.md`), and it sits alongside the
paper's own caution that shared sound values do not give access to Linear A's grammar or
vocabulary. Steele and Meißner do not use the word circular themselves.
