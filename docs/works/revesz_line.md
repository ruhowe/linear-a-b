# Revesz (2016), *West-Ugric / Uralic readings of Linear A; AIDA database*

WSEAS, IDEAS, MDPI Information. This entry covers a line of Revesz's papers: "Bioinformatics
Evolutionary Tree Algorithms Reveal the History of the Cretan Script Family" (2016,
comparing nine scripts including Old Hungarian); "Establishing the West-Ugric Language
Family with Minoan, Hattic and Hungarian by a Decipherment of Linear A," *WSEAS
Transactions on Information Science and Applications* 14 (2017), pp. 306-335, read in
full from the open PDF at wseas.org; the AIDA database (Revesz, Rashid and Tuyishime,
IDEAS 2019); and "Archaeogenetic Data Mining Supports a Uralic-Minoan Homeland in the
Danube Basin," *Information* (MDPI) 15(10):646 (2024). A separate 2024 paper in the same
MDPI journal's Revesz-edited special issue, "Minoan Cryptanalysis" by Nepal and Perono
Cacciafoco, is a different work by different authors and is catalogued separately
(`nepal2024`), not part of this entry.

## What it claims

Linear A encodes a Uralic/'West-Ugric' language, with values derived from visual resemblance to later alphabets.

## Our assessment

Verification **Y**. We fetched and read the primary source (the 2017 WSEAS paper) in full and found the passages that support the page.

Values from visual resemblance to unrelated alphabets a millennium later (Old Hungarian, Carian), then consonantal matching to a self-built dictionary. No null model. Computer-science venues only; no Aegean-epigraphy engagement.

Checked 2026-09-13: read the full text of the 2017 WSEAS paper. It confirms every element
of the existing assessment directly. Sign values come from a "feature-based similarity
measure" matched by eye against the Carian alphabet and the Old Hungarian alphabet (Tables
1-6), both attested more than a thousand years after Linear A. Vocabulary matches are built
by comparing Minoan word skeletons against Hungarian and other Uralic words drawn from "the
Hungarian etymological dictionary of Zaicz." A full-text search of the paper for "random",
"chance", "null", "baseline", "control" and "significan-" returns no matches: there is no
null model, random baseline or control corpus of any kind, consistent with the page's
existing claim. Confirmed the 2016 script-comparison paper and the 2024 MDPI paper are both
genuinely Revesz's; confirmed the distinct "Minoan Cryptanalysis" paper in the same MDPI
issue is by other authors (Nepal and Perono Cacciafoco) and is a separate catalogue entry,
not this one. Flag raised from S to Y on the strength of the 2017 paper; the 2016 script
paper, the AIDA database and the 2024 archaeogenetics paper remain unread beyond secondary
description.
