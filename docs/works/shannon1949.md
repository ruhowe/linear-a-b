# Shannon (1949), *Communication Theory of Secrecy Systems*

Bell System Technical Journal 28(4):656–715.

## What it claims

Defines unicity distance: the ciphertext length past which a substitution cipher's plaintext is in principle uniquely determined.

## Our assessment

Verification **Y**. We fetched and read the primary source.

The formal name for why a ~7,500-sign corpus with no parallel text and an unknown language may be under-determined regardless of method quality.

Checked 2026-09-13: read the full OCR text of Bell System Technical Journal volume 28 from
archive.org. Added the DOI, which was previously recorded as not found. The claim that the
paper defines unicity distance as the ciphertext length past which a substitution cipher's
plaintext is in principle uniquely determined is confirmed by the paper's own abstract:
"As N increases the field usually narrows down until eventually there is a unique
'solution' to the cryptogram," and "with ordinary languages and the usual types of ciphers
... this 'unicity distance' is approximately H(K)/D," worked out at about 30 letters for
simple substitution with a random key. Nothing on the page needed correction.
