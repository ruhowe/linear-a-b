## The facts that decide method choice

- Linear B is deciphered (1952). Its live problems are palaeography and scribal identity, Knossos chronology, 14 undeciphered syllabograms, dozens of unread logograms, restoration of damaged text, metrology. Nothing published: a Mycenaean language model, an LLM evaluation, sign OCR, join-finding, an automatic lemmatiser.
- Linear A has no accepted decipherment and no accepted language. The one peer-reviewed computational result *about Linear A itself* is the fraction-value system (Corazza et al. 2021). Every other computational attempt is dictionary matching on conventional values and reports itself inconclusive.
- Every automatic decipherment that has worked (Ugaritic, Linear B, Gothic, Iberian) assumed a known or candidate related language with substantial cognate share, segmented word lists, and an external anchor (names, a bilingual). Linear A supplies segmentation only.
- The Linear B benchmark (67–89% cognate accuracy) presupposes Ventris's sign values. It is cognate matching, not sign-value discovery, and says nothing about what is possible for Linear A.
- Sign values on Linear A: 25 demonstrably shared with B, ~60 more assumed on shape, 64/89 shapes shared. The o-series is nearly absent (2.9% of vowels). A language claim built on Linear B values is circular: this repo's position. Steele & Meißner 2017 argue the projection is legitimate in principle and leaves the language unknown; the circularity wording is ours (attribution corrected 2026-09-13).
- Entropy, Zipf, positional and hapax statistics detect organisation, not language. Matched non-linguistic controls and permutation nulls are mandatory (Sproat 2014; Raghavendra 2026; Nair 2026). Packard 1974 already ran random-reassignment nulls on Linear A.
- Scale gap: Ithaca 78,608 inscriptions, Aeneas 176,861 / 16M chars, versus DĀMOS 5,932 docs / 54,476 tokens and Linear A 1,534 inscriptions / 7,574 signs. The only published small-data restoration line (Patras BiRNN) reaches top-1 30–48%.
- **Published restoration figures on Linear B are weaker than they look, measured here 2026-09-10** ([linearb-restoration.md](linearb-restoration.md)): on a 75-sign candidate set, top-20 admits 27% of the inventory and a bare frequency prior already scores **63.2%**, against published top-20 of 66.2% and 78–80%. A Witten-Bell sign bigram from `aegean.analysis.surprisal` reaches the published range with no new modelling code, and a lexicon lookup beats it by 21.3 points on top-1. Both collapse on unseen word types (89.3% → 4.3%; 55.4% → 10.4%), so the aggregate mostly measures recall of a recurring place-name gazetteer. A single 80/20 split carries ±5 points of top-1 variance. **Report any top-k on a small signary against its frequency floor, and stratified by seen/unseen, or it will be over-read.**
- Linear A words are 83.9% hapax types (M). Distribution-only cross-script sign alignment recovers nothing above chance (pyaegean Procrustes, M).
- The field's own next steps: a unified machine-readable dataset with candidate-language lexica (Braović 2024); sign-inventory and allograph questions from images plus context (Corazza 2022 template); syllabotactics with a deciphered-script control (Davis 2026); numerals, fractions, transaction structure that need no sound values.
- None of the 2025–26 "AI deciphered Linear A" claims is peer-reviewed in an Aegean venue, but they are not all the same thing and this file should not flatten them. The Revesz line and the Zenodo self-publications fail the criteria above outright. **Di Mino 2026 does not**: read directly on 2026-09-10, it engages the circularity objection rather than ignoring it, cites the specialist literature accurately, and was discussed with a Rutgers Semitist. It is untested rather than refuted — 67% of its central inscription rests on demonstrably-shared values (measured here) — and it is the single most testable claim in circulation. See the language-hypotheses table and the hype register for the distinction.

Secure structure, independent of language:

- Transaction terms: KU-RO 37 tokens, KI-RO 16, PO-TO-KU-RO 2 (M); KU-RO = total is confirmed arithmetically on the 37 tablets with checkable sums (pyaegean `accounting.py`). Hooker's Semitic-loan reading of ku-ro is noted, not accepted.
- Libation formula: A-TA-I-*301-WA-JA (11 tokens, M) · (J)A-SA-SA-RA-ME · U-NA-KA-NA-SI · I-PI-NA-MA · SI-RU-TE in fixed slots on stone vessels; first word is a verb with root -i-*301- and varying affixes (Davis 2013; Monti 2022).
- Fractions: a coherent system whose lowest value is 1/60; the LA 1/10 sign survives as an LB dry-measure unit (Corazza et al. 2021).
- Toponyms shared with LB: pa-i-to, se-to-i-ja (Y); su-ki-ri-ta, ku-do-ni (standard, S).
- Vowel profile: o nearly absent; whether LA had e/o series is open, "gaps may be accidental given ~7,100 signs" (Meißner & Steele).
- Corpus composition (M, pyaegean load): 6,406 tokens = 2,211 logograms + 1,621 numerals + 1,381 words + 669 unknown + 524 separators. Only ~1.4k tokens are text at all.
- Word statistics (M): 995 word types; **835 hapax = 83.9% of types, 60.5% of tokens**; TTR 0.72; mean 2.92 signs/word (629 two-sign, 446 three-sign). Within-word signs: 4,038 tokens, 162 types (56 hapax); unigram H = 5.90 bits, bigram H(X₂|X₁) = 3.88 bits (MLE, biased low; 713 of 1,185 bigram types seen once).
- Same measures on DĀMOS for scale (M): 14,491 word tokens, 7,867 types, 79.1% hapax types / 42.9% of tokens, TTR 0.54, mean 3.22 signs/word; H 6.62 / 4.61 bits on raw labels (774 label types, unnormalised for damage marks). LB is only ~10× LA and is itself hapax-heavy.
- Haghia Triada supplies ~147 tablets (S), most of the administrative corpus; sites are few, so site-grouped splits leave almost nothing to hold out.

## Requirements for a credible contribution, as the field states them

- A claim about language needs a known or candidate related language, a substantial cognate share, and an anchor outside the statistics (Snyder 2010; Luo 2019, 2021; Tamburini 2025; Zender via Mair 2026).
- Sign values may not be imported by convention when the claim is about language: this repo's rule, consistent with Steele & Meißner 2017, who defend the projection of values in principle and hold that it leaves the language unknown. Nepal & Perono Cacciafoco 2024 attempt "bias correction"; Luo 2019's LB result simply inherits Ventris.
- Any structural statistic must beat matched non-linguistic controls and a permutation null (Sproat 2014; Rao 2010 reply; Nair 2026; Raghavendra 2026). Packard 1974's fictitious decipherments are the LA-specific precedent.
- Validation protocol (Raghavendra 2026): commit sign values and semantic predictions before testing; succeed on grouped held-out inscriptions; prefer one language over matched alternatives; stable values across resamples; predict external archaeological or textual facts.
- Human in the loop (Tamburini 2025; Ithaca/Aeneas historian-in-loop design). Ground truth is "unattainable"; evaluate against expert assumptions (Sommerschield 2023 §9).
- Prefer questions that need no sound values: numerals and fractions (Corazza 2021), sign inventory and allography (Corazza 2022; Ferrara et al. Kadmos 2021), document structure and transaction terms (Younger), syllabotactics with a deciphered control (Davis 2026).
- Quote edition counts, not loader counts, and record which "shared sign" basis is used (corpus-sources.md).

## Open problems the field states, and what to INVESTIGATE here

Field-stated:

- Linear B: 14 undeciphered syllabograms (Judson 2020); dozens of unread logograms (two read in 2025); Knossos single vs multiple destructions (Driessen; Firth & Skelton 2016; Pierini, Mouthuy 2024); whether palaeography can date at all (Salgarella & Judson 2024); scribal training and identity (Judson 2022; Giorgi 2026); metrology (Killen 2024 reviews); interoperability of digital editions (Aurora 2025); no benchmarks for computation (Papavassileiou 2024; Braović 2024).
- Linear A: vowel inventory / o-series (Meißner & Steele); LA→LB continuity vs reform (Salgarella 2020 vs Nash 2021); CH↔LA sign equations and CH unity (Meissner & Salgarella 2024; Ferrara et al. 2021); one language or several (Monti 2022); validity of syllabotactic method (Donnelly 2026); SigLA lacks non-administrative texts, transliteration, Unicode (Salgarella & Castellan); corpus below Barber's provability threshold for an isolate (Barber 1974 via Language Log, 24 May 2023; no number found, see claims.md C-5a).
- Computational: unified dataset with candidate-language lexica, augmentation (Braović 2024 §9); apply optimisation decipherment to Aegean scripts once a candidate language is testable (Tamburini 2025); sign-inventory reclassification for LA/CH by image + context (Corazza 2022 template); language-internal typology with deciphered controls (Davis 2026).

Ru delegated the confirm-or-strike call to Claude's judgement from this survey's findings, explicitly weighing the July 2026 Di Mino "Linear A is Semitic" failure (Fringe and hype register, above) as the precedent to guard against — an unreviewed decipherment claim built with this same class of AI tooling.

| Decision | Date | Reason | Rejected alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dictionary matching (the etymological method) is not used to test any language claim about Linear A until a version separates a right real language from a wrong real language on Linear B by more than the sample spread | 2026-09-11 | It returns a result for every language at the same strength as the right one, on Linear B where the right one is known (FINDINGS F-008, F-013, F-020). More corpus would not change this | Refining maps or lexicons further; the failure is shape matching shape, not power |
| Structural analysis (the Kober method) is the project's line on Linear A, read against a size-matched Linear B floor | 2026-09-11 | It recovers Mycenaean grammar from Linear B with no sound values (F-016) and gives one true statement about Linear A at its corpus size (F-019, F-020). Its limit is power, which is measurable | Reading its Linear A output against the full Linear B result, which would over-read it |
| The 919-pair LB↔Greek set is a benchmark to compare against, never training or published data | 2026-09-10 | No licence; derived from a CC BY-ND lexicon; inherits Ventris. Mirrored as a Decision in corpus-sources.md | Reusing it as the project's parallel corpus |
| Every LA structure statistic ships with a permutation null **and** a non-linguistic control | 2026-09-10 | Sproat 2014; SIGIL 2026; Packard 1974 already set this precedent for Linear A specifically | Bare numbers, which reviewers now reject on sight |
| No language-affiliation claim from conventionally-projected sign values without a null model priced in | 2026-09-10 | The circularity argument, which is this repo's (Steele & Meißner 2017 defend projection in principle and say it leaves the language unknown). The rule is *not* that projection is illegitimate — Meißner & Steele's own Table 1 licenses 25 signs. It is that a claim resting partly on unsecured values (Di Mino 2026 measures at 67% secured on IO Za 2) needs its degrees of freedom priced by a null before it counts as evidence. Reinforces the invariant in corpus-sources.md | Asserting affiliation from a coherent-looking reading alone, which permissive root-matching will produce whether or not the hypothesis is true; equally, dismissing such a claim without running the null |
| Default priority order is value-free questions first: numerals/fractions, sign inventory and allography, transaction structure, LB restoration | 2026-09-10 | The only peer-reviewed computational LA result is value-free (Corazza 2021); LB restoration has a public CC BY 4.0 baseline to build on (Zenodo 7404653). Not a permanent ban — a genuine comparative anchor would change this | Starting with decipherment-flavoured work before the value-free groundwork exists |
| Quote edition counts (Linear A 1,534 docs / 7,574 signs; DĀMOS 5,932 docs), and name the shared-sign basis used (81 / 56 / 53 — see corpus-sources.md) | 2026-09-10 | Loader counts (pyaegean's 1,721) are a mirror artefact, not the edition | Quoting 1,721 as "the corpus" |
